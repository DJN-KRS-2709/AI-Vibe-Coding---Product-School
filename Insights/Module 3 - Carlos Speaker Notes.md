# Module 3: The Precision Shift — Speaker Notes for Carlos

> For Carlos only. High-level bullets explaining what we're doing and why.

---

## Where M3 Sits

- M1 = speed, M2 = aim, **M3 = precision**
- Students stop asking AI to build → start **directing** AI like sprint tickets
- The hinge of the course: after M3, the prototype looks like a real product → makes M4–M6 possible
- Kills the "start over" instinct — teaches expand, add states, refine

---

## The M3 Wow (Two Beats)

- **Beat 1:** Loading skeletons + error states appear → same screens suddenly behave like shipped software
- **Beat 2:** Student changes ONE screen, nothing else breaks → "I'm steering, not restarting"
- **Crescendo:** Triple reveal — M1 / M2 / M3 side by side in three tabs. Undeniable visual progression.

---

## Opening (Slides 1–2)

- Hook: "Your M2 proves something. Would your VP ship it?"
- Respects M2 work while naming the gap: not enough screens, no states, not reproducible
- Creates pull for the lab — they WANT the toolkit

---

## Demo (Slides 3–5)

- Instructor pulls up the actual M2 Retention Engine
- Runs 3 prompts live, sequentially:
  - **Expand** → 3 screens become 5 (adds team workspace + PM dashboard)
  - **Behavior** → loading skeletons, error handling, empty states
  - **Refine** → iterates PM dashboard without rebuilding anything else
- Fast — 5 min total, pre-typed prompts, paste instantly
- Speed IS the point

---

## Mini Activity (Slide 6)

- Students diagnose their own M2: how many screens? any states? reproducible?
- Gap-surfacing BEFORE teaching — they feel the problem first, then want the solution
- Fixes Carlos's "I'm leaving not excited" feedback from old structure

---

## Teaching (Slides 7–10)

- Compressed to ~12 min (old M3 was ~40 min of slides)
- Four concepts, kept light:
  - Prompting maturity curve (exploration → execution)
  - Prompt chain anatomy (Expand / Behavior / Refine)
  - Multi-step prompting techniques
  - Living Prompt Packs — reusable toolkit that grows
- 55% hands-on ratio (old was 29%) — directly from Carlos's "fireworks all the time" feedback

---

## Lab (Slides 11–12) — THE FIREWORKS

- 30 min, two parts
- **Part 1 (8 min):** Plan in the Prompt Chain Planner BEFORE opening Lovable
  - Forces documentation → chain itself becomes the deliverable
- **Part 2 (22 min):** Execute 3 prompts against their own M2 build
  - Wow happens in STUDENTS' hands, not instructor's
  - Walk the room — watch for loading skeleton reactions and the "I didn't start over" moment
- Key instruction: "Don't rebuild. Expand what you have."

---

## Peer Review (Slide 13)

- Partner follows your chain without seeing the build first
- Can they understand each step? Could they reproduce it?
- Validates: the chain is portable, not just personal
- Second wow beat: partner gets a similar result from just the documentation

---

## Break It (Slide 15)

- When a chain goes off-rails: diagnose before re-prompting
- Read → compare → find the divergence → fix ONE prompt
- Establishes debugging instinct needed for M5/M6

---

## Triple Reveal (Slide 16)

- Three browser tabs: M1 | M2 | M3
- Let students open them. 30 seconds of silence. Don't narrate.
- "Same tool. Same you."
- Sets up M4's four-tab, M5's five-tab, M6's final showcase

---

## Tools

- **Prompt Chain Planner** — used DURING lab. Forces plan-before-build. Scenario auto-populate.
- **Living Prompt Pack Builder** — used AFTER lab. Long-term asset. Grows through M4–M6.
- Together: plan → build → document → reuse

---

## How It Addresses Carlos's Feedback

- **"Every module should have fireworks"** → Triple reveal + wow in students' hands
- **"Fireworks all the time"** → 55% hands-on, compressed teaching
- **"I'm leaving not excited"** → Gap-surfacing before teaching creates urgency
- **"Advanced prompt chains"** → Expand / Behavior / Refine framework, peer-validated
- **"Look and feel matching"** → Builds on M2's design matching, preserves design language across new screens
