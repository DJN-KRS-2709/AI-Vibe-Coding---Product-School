### Tool Strategy: Lovable as THE Tool
- **Finalized partnership with Lovable**: free credits for 3 months for all students
- **Use Lovable as the primary and only demo tool** -- don't switch between Bolt and Lovable, it's too confusing
- **Framework is tool-agnostic** in principle: teach the methodology so students can apply it to any tool at their company. But in class, we use Lovable exclusively.
- Briefly mention competitive landscape ("there are tools out there, your company might use different ones, what you learn here applies to any tool") but don't demo alternatives
- Other partnerships exist: Alloy, Magic Patterns, others -- mention as resources for curious students, not as course tools

### Group Work: Individual Builds, Optional Group Activities
- **No enforced group projects or group presentations**
- **Every student must build something individually** -- this is non-negotiable, the accountability metric
- **Group activities during class are great**: pair programming, comparing notes, discussions, networking. But groups don't need to be the same every session -- mix it up.
- **No homework or between-class group obligations** -- don't assume students have time outside of class
- **Final day**: everyone has built an individual product. Optional demo for brave volunteers. Dejan gives feedback to those who present. Others benefit from watching.
- **Group = accountability, not dependency**: "It's like showing up to the gym. You feel pressure to show something because your peers are showing something. But you're not building the same thing together."

### Pre-Prototyping Must Be More Robust
- Carlos wants to see: how do you make prototypes look and feel like your EXISTING product?
- **Figma integrations, MCP servers, design system cloning** -- show students they can basically clone their existing product in a prototyped environment
- **Templates**: Use Mobbin and similar template libraries so students don't start from scratch. Feed existing product design into the AI tool and focus only on the new feature.
- **High fidelity, not basic demos**: "Not just the basic 'create an e-commerce shop that sells whatever.' This has to be much more robust."
- Carlos specifically said: "How are you making sure the look and feel is as similar as possible to your current product?"

### Post-Prototyping Must Be More Robust
- Carlos: "There's no excuse. Lovable and these tools are developing backend functionalities to allow people to ship products. They don't want to say prototyping anymore."
- **Show students they can**: connect to a database, import from Cloud, connect to Stripe, push to production
- **Mention software engineering agents** (Devin, etc.) that can do testing, integrate on Slack -- show the magic of "this is real, not just playing designer"
- The "Ship It" moment needs to feel like actual production, not just a localhost demo

### Course Must Feel ADVANCED
- Carlos: "I want the opposite [of too basic]. I want people complaining because this is too advanced. That means you're pushing their limits."
- "Everybody that shows up to this class has done some basic prototyping before. You're here to show them how to make this work for real, for their company."
- Every module must include building. No full session of theory before touching the tool.
