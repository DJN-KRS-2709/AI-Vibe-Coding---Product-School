## Key Decisions and Feedback

### Opening and Confidence Line
- **Keep it simple.** Carlos: "The first AI road type in glass—module one instructor tried to give too much detail and students weren't ready. It's the first day, they don't even know what they don't know."
- **One powerful line:** "You are going to be able to build your own applications end to end. Boom. That is fantastic. I don't think there is much more to add at this point."
- No theory. Complete a full build-show-learn cycle. That's the selling point.

### Ground Rules
- **All action.** Carlos: "Creating it in a way that is well received instead of oh, it's a list of things I'll never remember. Boom. All action."
- **Add "Your Project":** Make it explicit that they will build and present their application. That was never clear in the first session—people came back with lots of questions.
- **Individual final project.** Tons of group activities during class, but the final project is on them. No group dependencies.
- **No homework between sessions.** Plenty to explore, but not required.

### Final Project Requirements
- **Product School to provide:** One slide + 3-minute video walking students through expectations, requirements, FAQ
- **When:** Early in the course (e.g., after ground rules)
- **Why video:** "Anytime we just say, hey, go look at this slide or read this brief, they don't do it. If we play a video, it's the only time we have their full attention."

### Terminology (Consistent Across Product School Courses)
- **Demo** = instructor building live
- **Hands-on lab** = students building in the tool
- **Breakout group activity** = students in groups (e.g., swapping prototypes, filling templates)
- Carlos: "Get rid of 'lab' when it's ambiguous. Demo is for instructor. When students do something, use a different label."

### The Tools Section (New, ~5 Minutes)
- **Juicy topic.** Carlos: "This is going to require a couple of slides or at least five minutes. People really like this."
- **Sell the choice:** "There are a ton of tools out there. We're using the best right now in the market. We have a partnership with Lovable, so you can use it for free. We have additional partnerships with Replit, v0, Magic Patterns. Let me explain why we're picking this over that. You're all welcome to explore different tools as you go."
- **Credits heads-up:** Lovable gives ~100 credits/month. "Do the planning in Gemini or ChatGPT—we have a code for free ChatGPT Plus—and then once you have the prompt, paste it on Lovable. That alone will save you the trouble of someone raising their hand and saying, hey Dejan, I already ran out of credits."
- Dana: "Someone will run out of credits in Module 1 if we don't mention this."

### Breakout Rooms
- **Don't suffer.** Carlos: "Given that the final project is individual, I really don't care who is in those breakout rooms. Just tell me how many people you want in each room. Press the button. That's it. Don't suffer."
- Random assignment is fine. No need to assign people carefully.

### Share Formats
- **First share (What Surprised You):** Full-class discussion. Everyone in main room. Instructor moderates.
- **Second share (Pair/Group Review):** Breakout rooms. Smaller groups comparing notes.

### Design Systems
- **Meaty topic.** Carlos: "People after that initial aha moment—we need to decompose what makes an excellent prompt. What is the right template? How do you import your design system if it's on Figma? How do you get inspiration for the right patterns? Mention Mobbin—it's free. Check out examples of pricing page, checkout flow. We should also talk about how easy it is to run out of credits."
- Module 2 will cover in more detail. Module 1 is the intro.

### Wrap
- **Add what's next.** Carlos: "I think we should add as part of that what we are going to cover next time."
