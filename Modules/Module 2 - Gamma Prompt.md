## Slide 5 — The Problem Framework
**The Problem Frame** (Reforge-adapted)

Six elements. Shown here filled out for the Retention Engine:

- **Goal** — Reduce 90-day churn from 30% to 15%
- **Problem** — Solo users churn at 3x the rate of team users
- **Context** — B2B PM tool, 5K teams, Series A
- **Constraints** — No engineering for 6 weeks, must work within existing product
- **Success Criteria** — Day-3 invite rate increases from 12% to 25%
- **Explore** — Is the blocker discoverability or motivation?

The last element — *Explore* — is the key. What are you LEARNING by building this?

## Slide 6 — Risk Types + Kill Switch
**What's Your Kill Switch?**

Three risk categories — your risk type determines what you build:

| Risk | The Question | Build This |
|---|---|---|
| **Value** | Will anyone want this? | Landing page |
| **Usability** | Will the experience make sense? | Clickable mockup |
| **Feasibility** | Will the logic actually work? | Functional app |

**The kill-switch question:** Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing? Build to test THAT one first.

0-to-1: the kill switch is usually desirability.
1-to-N: the kill switch is usually adoption.

## Slide 7 — The Validation Loop
**The Validation Loop**

The cycle you'll run in every lab from now on:

1. **Identify the Hypothesis** — the "why" behind every prompt
2. **Define the Constraints** — the guardrails for the build
3. **Build and Stress-Test** — find where it BREAKS, not where it works
4. **Synthesize and Pivot** — update your hypothesis with what you learned

Step 3 is the key: you're not building to see it work. You're building to see where it breaks.

*Validation Brief template is in Slack. Fill it out in Lab 1.*

## Slide 8 — Prompt Anatomy
**What Makes an Excellent Prompt**

Think of this as *configuring a builder tool*, not chatting with an AI.

**Context** — "Build a retention dashboard for a B2B PM tool used by 5K teams."

**Reference** — Attach a screenshot. "Match this look and feel."

**Constraint** — "Prioritize the invite flow. Avoid generic patterns. Max 3 screens."

**Output** — "A 3-screen flow: invite prompt, team setup wizard, collaborative task view."

The more specific the configuration, the better the output.

## Slide 11 — Lab Part 2: Build With Context
**Hands-on Lab Part 2: Build With Context** | 25 Minutes

Open Lovable. Build your prompt with all four inputs:

1. Paste your **scenario brief** as context. Include 2-3 user quotes.
2. Attach your **screenshot** as a reference.
3. Add **specific metrics**: "Display these numbers: [X], [Y], [Z]."
4. Define the **output**: "Build a [N]-screen flow that tests [your hypothesis]."

**Your goal:** Build something that looks like a real product AND tests a specific hypothesis.

**Not your goal:** Make it pretty.

Verify your shareable link works — you're swapping in 25 minutes.

## Slide 12 — Peer Review: Does This Test It?
**Breakout Group Activity: Does This Test It?** | 10 Minutes

Breakout rooms. Groups of 2.

**3 minutes of silence.** Explore your partner's prototype cold. No explanation.

**Then discuss:**
- Does this prototype actually test the stated hypothesis?
- Where does the experience break beyond the happy path?
- Can you see real data in here? Does it make the prototype more credible?
- Tool vs. Toy — would this help the builder make a decision, or does it just look good?

The question isn't "is this cool?" It's "does this test what you said you were testing?"
