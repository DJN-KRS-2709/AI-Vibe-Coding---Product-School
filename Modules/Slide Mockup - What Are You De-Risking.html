What Are You De-Risking?

A prototype is a strategic experiment. Your hypothesis identifies the riskiest part of your product concept — which tells you exactly where to focus your build.
If your risk is Value
"Will users actually rely on this for a critical task?"
Focus on user intent. Test whether the core premise solves a real problem — or is just a novelty.
If your risk is Usability
"Will the interface feel like a tool or an overwhelming black box?"
Focus on interaction. Simplify complex steps, find where the flow breaks down.
If your risk is Feasibility
"Can the logic generate accurate results from these inputs?"
Focus on constraints. Find edge cases where your logic or data might fail.

Speaker Notes

Every prototype is a strategic experiment. Before you build, ask: "What is the single riskiest assumption I need to validate?"

If your risk is value: You're testing whether anyone actually wants this. Focus on the core premise — does the output solve a real problem or is it a novelty?

If your risk is usability: You're testing whether the experience makes sense. Focus on interaction — where does the flow break down?

If your risk is feasibility: You're testing whether the logic actually works. Focus on constraints and edge cases — what inputs break it?

For greenfield (0-to-1): Start with value. For existing products (1-to-N): Start with adoption or integration risk.

You'll choose your risk focus in the next exercise.
