Teaching MODIFY — was M2 slide 10
Bridging Design and Build — Three Entry Points
Modern vibe coding tools are multimodal. They can ingest your existing assets and match your design system. Here are the three entry points:

1. Text-to-App — Natural language prompts. Best for new concepts where no design exists. This is what you did in M1.

2. Screenshot-to-App — Upload a screenshot from Mobbin, your product, or a competitor. The tool matches it. You used this to refine in M1 Lab 2. Today you'll use it as a starting input alongside real data.

3. Design-to-Code — Figma imports or design system docs. For on-brand prototypes from existing design systems. Figma now supports code-to-design too — the boundary is dissolving in both directions.

The strategic choice: Which entry point depends on what assets you have. 0-to-1 products usually start with Text-to-App. 1-to-N products should start with Screenshot-to-App or Design-to-Code — you never start from scratch when you already have a product.

Framework MODIFY — was slide 25, compressed
Name It Before You Build It
Before you touch the tool, name the test. One sentence:
"I'm testing whether [X]."

If you can't finish that sentence, you're not ready to build.

The Validation Loop (the cycle you'll run from now on):
  • 1. Identify the Hypothesis — the "why" behind every prompt
  • 2. Define the Constraints — the guardrails for the build
  • 3. Build and Stress-Test — find where it BREAKS, not where it works
  • 4. Synthesize and Pivot — update your hypothesis with what you learned
3 min
Speaker Notes

Earned now. They just felt the gap. This framework is the fix. "Four steps. Step 3 is the one people skip: you're not building to see it work. You're building to see where it breaks."

Framework MODIFY — was slides 26 + Kill Switch mockup
What's Your Kill Switch?
Three risk categories — your risk type determines what you build:

Value → Will anyone want this? → Build a landing page
Usability → Will the experience make sense? → Build a clickable mockup
Feasibility → Will the logic actually work? → Build a functional app

The kill-switch question: Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing?

0-to-1: the kill switch is usually desirability.
1-to-N: the kill switch is usually adoption.

Speaker Notes

Use the Kill Switch slide mockup design. "Match fidelity to risk. The Retention Engine scenario — that's 1-to-N, the product exists. Your kill switch is adoption. You don't need to prove people want it. You need to prove they'll use the invite flow."

Hands-On Lab — Part 2: Build (THE FIREWORKS) MODIFY — was slide 32 + Gamma slide 11
Assemble the Prompt, Paste Into Lovable, See the Difference
Individual Exercise | 20 minutes
This is the core activity. Follow the workflow:

Step 1 — Open your prompt builder (ChatGPT or Claude). This is where you assemble, not Lovable.

Step 2 — Paste your context. Drop in your scenario brief from the Context Data Pack. Include 2-3 real user quotes directly in the prompt.

Step 3 — Upload your screenshot. Attach the visual reference. "Match this look and feel."

Step 4 — Set your guardrails from the Validation Brief. Add your hypothesis, constraints, and specific metrics: "Display these numbers: [X], [Y], [Z]." Define the output: "Build a [N]-screen flow that tests [your hypothesis]."

Step 5 — Copy the assembled prompt into Lovable. Paste. Hit build. Watch the result.

This is the moment. Your prototype should come back with real data, real user language, and a specific hypothesis embedded. Not lorem ipsum. Not generic charts. Your users' words. Your metrics. Your question being tested.

Verify your shareable link works — you're swapping prototypes soon.
20 min build

Walk the room. (1) People who went straight to Lovable without the prompt builder — "Step back, assemble first." (2) People stuck assembling — help them sequence the four inputs. (3) People who finished early — "Where does it break beyond the happy path? What would a skeptical VP ask?"

Breakout Group Activity KEEP — was slide 33
Peer Review: Does This Test It?
Breakout rooms. Groups of 2. | 10 minutes

3 minutes of silence. Explore your partner's prototype cold. No explanation.

Then discuss:
  • Does this prototype actually test the stated hypothesis?
  • Where does the experience break beyond the happy path?
  • Can you see real data in here? Does it make the prototype more credible?
  • Tool vs. Toy — would this help the builder make a decision, or does it just look good?

The question isn't "is this cool?" It's "does this test what you said you were testing?"
10 min
Speaker Notes

Enforce the silence. "3 minutes. Don't explain your prototype. If they can't tell what you're testing from using it, that's a signal."

Evolution from M1: In M1, the peer review was "what confused you about this prototype?" In M2, it's "does this test what you claim?" The bar has moved from clarity to validation.
