Live Build: 3 Prompts
The Graduation Chain
PROMPT 1 — EXTRACT
"Look at this entire prototype. Write a product requirements document that covers: what this product does, who it's for, the problem it solves, the 5 screens and their purpose, the user flow from first screen to last, the hypothesis it tests, the key metrics, what's currently mocked vs. what would need real data, and recommended next steps for engineering."
PROMPT 2 — REFACTOR
"Refactor the codebase. Rename all components to be descriptive (e.g., OnboardingWelcome, TeamInviteForm, PMDashboard). Separate data logic from display components. Group files by feature, not by type. Add a README.md that explains the project structure."
PROMPT 3 — HANDOFF
"Generate an engineering handoff document: list every component and what it does, describe the data model (what's real vs. mocked), identify the 3 biggest technical decisions an engineer would need to make, and write a 'start here' guide."

Then: 3 prompts. A spec, structured code, and an engineering handoff.
5 min
Speaker Notes

Switch to code view. Show the before/after. "Component1 is now OnboardingWelcome." Prompt 3: paste, wait ~40 sec. Read the handoff highlights. Total: ~7 minutes.

Graduation Judgment
When Do You Stop Exploring and Start Building For Real?
Graduate when: hypothesis validated, stakeholders said "build this", you know what's real vs. mocked, prototype answers a specific question with evidence.

Don't graduate when: still exploring direction, testing multiple things, haven't shown anyone, can't explain the hypothesis.

The graduation moment isn't about the code being ready. It's about the question being answered.
3 min
Speaker Notes

"This is the judgment call that separates PMs who prototype from PMs who ship." Graduation isn't a code quality bar — it's a knowledge bar. Ask 2-3 students: "Based on your M3 prototype, would you graduate today?"

8
Comprehension Debt
Comprehension Debt — The Silent Killer
Technical debt has a cousin: comprehension debt. Every prompt adds features, every feature adds complexity. After 50+ prompts, even YOU can't explain what your prototype does without clicking through it.

Signs: "Let me show you" (can't describe without demo), prototype does things you didn't ask for, can't explain the data model, new prompts break old things.

The fix: Extract the spec. The Living PRD is your comprehension debt payoff.
3 min
Speaker Notes

"Comprehension debt is when the prototype knows more than you do." The Living PRD forces you to articulate what you've built.

Breakout Group Activity
Breakout Group Activity: Can You Build From This? | 10 Minutes
Groups of 2. The test: Person A shares ONLY their Living PRD (not the prototype). Person B reads it and answers:
  • What does this product do?
  • Who is it for?
  • What hypothesis does it test?
  • What's real vs. mocked?
  • What should engineering build first?

If Person B can answer all 5, it's handoff-ready.
10 min
Speaker Notes

Enforce: NO showing the prototype. PRD only. "If your partner can describe the product from just reading your PRD — you've written a spec that stands on its own." Watch for the reaction when it works: the student realizes their prototype can exist independently of them. That's graduation.
