Framework

The Validation Lens Loop.

This cycle turns raw ideas into validated strategy.

Before touching any tool, name the test: if you can't complete the sentence "I'm testing whether [X]," you aren't ready to build.

Each revolution sharpens your hypothesis and tightens constraints — moving your product from uncertain to evidence-backed.

Most people prototype in a straight line. Vibe Coding requires a circle — the loop is the upgrade.
Framework

What's your kill switch?

Your hypothesis identifies the riskiest part. Your kill switch forces clarity about what to build and test first.

Ask one question: "Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing?"
💡
Risk · Value
Will anyone actually want this?
→ Build a landing page
Why: tests whether the core premise solves a real problem or is just a novelty. Sign-ups and click-throughs are your kill-switch evidence.
Kill-switch signal
Conversion below your threshold → premise was wrong, pivot the offer.
👆
Risk · Usability
Will the experience make sense?
→ Build a clickable mockup
Why: simplifies complex steps and surfaces where the flow breaks down. Friction points are your kill-switch evidence.
Kill-switch signal
Stuck within 30s on the core action → re-architect the flow, not the visuals.
⚙️
Risk · Feasibility
Will the logic actually work?
→ Build a functional app
Why: finds edge cases where your logic or data might fail. Error rates are your kill-switch evidence.
Kill-switch signal
Logic falls over on basic inputs → escalate to eng for a feasibility spike.
0 → 1 · New Products
Kill switch: desirability.

Nobody wants it yet, so prove they will. You're testing whether the problem is real before you invest in building.

1 → N · Existing Products
Kill switch: adoption and flow.

People have it, now prove they'll use it. Screenshot what exists and build on top.

Key Takeaways

Validate Product Hypotheses via Risk-Based Prototyping

Product Managers must master the three ingredients of context injection: hypothesis, real data, and user voice, to build high-signal prototypes. This ensures the AI output reflects actual market conditions rather than a generic design.

PMs utilize the validation loop to move systematically through the stages of hypothesis, constraints, stress testing, and synthesis. This provides technical and usability evidence, compressing weeks of traditional development into a single session.

Product Managers should use the problem framework to articulate exactly what is being tested before wasting credits or prompting. Defining the what and why upfront prevents aimless building and ensures the resulting prototype serves as a precision instrument for validation.
