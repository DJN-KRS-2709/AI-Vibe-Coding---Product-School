Framework

The Validation Lens Loop.

Most people prototype in a straight line. Vibe Coding requires a circle — each revolution sharpens your hypothesis and tightens constraints.

01
Identify the Hypothesis
Name the test. If you can't complete the sentence "I'm testing whether [X]," you aren't ready to build.
02
Define the Constraints
Guardrails for the build — risk type, fidelity, technical limits. Context injection of data and user voice.
03
Build and Stress-Test
Don't build to look pretty. Build to find where it breaks. Finding a break = highest signal.
04
Synthesize and Pivot
Update your hypothesis with what you learned. Continue or pivot. Iterate strategy, not pixels.

Each revolution sharpens hypothesis · tightens constraints · compresses weeks of traditional dev into a single session.

Framework

What's your kill switch?

Your hypothesis identifies the riskiest part. Your kill switch forces clarity about what to build and test first.

Ask: "Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing?"

Risk TypeThe QuestionBuild ThisWhy
Value"Will anyone actually want this?"Landing pageTests whether the core premise solves a real problem or is just a novelty.
Usability"Will the experience make sense?"Clickable mockupSimplifies complex steps and surfaces where the flow breaks down.
Feasibility"Will the logic actually work?"Functional appFinds edge cases where your logic or data might fail.
0 → 1 · New Products
Kill switch: desirability.

Nobody wants it yet, so prove they will. You're testing whether the problem is real before you invest in building.

1 → N · Existing Products
Kill switch: adoption and flow.

People have it, now prove they'll use it. Screenshot what exists and build on top.

Final Project Scenario

Choose your scenario.

This is your commitment moment. The scenario you pick today is the one you carry through M2–M6. No switching after today.

01
The Retention Engine

LIKELY RISK · VALUE

B2B SaaS losing 30% of users in 90 days. Why are they leaving? Build a prototype that surfaces churn drivers and tests whether an intervention can change the curve.
02
The Internal Tool Nobody Uses

LIKELY RISK · USABILITY

CRM with 18% adoption. Built it, no one came. Figure out why adoption is so low and prototype a version that actually fits how people work. Classic 1→N problem.
03
The Marketplace Trust Problem

LIKELY RISK · VALUE / FEASIBILITY

Bookings flat, zero-review providers. Buyers don't trust new providers. Prototype a trust mechanism that gets first-time providers their first booking.
04
The Dashboard Nobody Reads

LIKELY RISK · USABILITY

Analytics with 60% bounce rate. Data exists but nobody finds insights. Prototype a version that surfaces the right information so people use it to make decisions.
★ Bring Your Own

Use a real problem from your own company. Check with your instructor for approval during the break — it needs a clear user, a measurable outcome, and enough ambiguity to test.

Framework

The Problem Framework · six-part checklist.

Articulate what you're testing, not just what you're shipping. Fill this out before you open any tool — every element sharpens your intent.

01
Goal
What are we trying to achieve?
The outcome, not the output.
02
Problem
What's blocking us right now?
Name the obstacle precisely.
03
Context
Who, where, when?
The specifics that shape the solution space.
04
Constraints
What can't we change?
Technical limits, timelines, budgets.
05
Success Criteria
What does validation look like?
The pass/fail threshold, upfront.
06
Explore
What are we trying to learn?
This is the key. The learning intent.

📝 Prototyping doesn't replace the PM responsibility of identifying the problem. It makes validation faster.

⏰ 15 min
Individual Exercise · 15 minutes

Frame before you build.

Pre-work to lock in strategy. Do NOT open Lovable yet. Use the Prototype Builder for the fast path or the Validation Brief for the deeper path — both end with the same assembled prompt.

  1. Complete your Validation Brief. Define your hypothesis, risk type, kill switch, and success criteria — your build is now designed to test a specific assumption.
  2. Execute context injection with the Context Data Pack for your scenario. Highlight 2–3 quotes that relate to your hypothesis. Pick 3–5 metrics you want the prototype to surface.
  3. Secure a visual reference — a Mobbin screenshot or your M1 prototype screenshot. Focus on the pattern (team-invite flow, analytics dashboard), not the product name.
  4. Smoke-test the assembled prompt in ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude — find logic holes before burning Lovable credits.

⏰ 40 min
Lab Exercise · 40 minutes

Start your final project and build with confidence.

Take your finalised prompt from the Prototype Builder into Lovable and trigger your first high-fidelity build. Solo build. Prove intent drives a more strategic prototype output.

  1. Open Lovable and paste your finalised prompt into a fresh project so you have a clear M1↔M2 comparison. Upload reference screenshots. Trigger the build.
  2. Review the output & iterate. Three checks: (a) do your real user quotes and metrics appear, or is it placeholder text? (b) can you actually test your kill switch by clicking through? (c) does it feel domain-specific or generic? Re-prompt targeted fixes.
  3. Connect to GitHub. GitHub icon in Lovable's top-right → "Connect to GitHub" → "Install & Authorize" → "Connect project". Then prompt: "Push all current project files to my connected GitHub repository."
  4. ⚠ Screenshot + generate your shareable link. You'll be paired with a NEW partner the moment the timer hits zero. You can't review a blank screen — have a functional link ready.

Breakout · 10 min · NEW partner

Show and swap your prototype.

Pair up with a NEW peer. Experience their build as a real user. Test whether strategic intent and kill switch actually translated into the build.

StepWhat to do
Swap links · new partnerDrop your Lovable share link in the new breakout chat. Grab your partner's. No verbal context given or received — let the build speak.
3 min silent walkClick as if you've never seen it. If they get stuck, let them stay stuck — that's your data. Take notes against the three credibility questions.
Discuss · then swap roles · 6 min totalWalk the three questions with your partner. Be specific — vague feedback doesn't move the build. Then swap roles and run the loop on yours.
The three credibility questions (verbatim from the legacy walkthrough)
  • What do you think the core hypothesis or kill switch was?
  • Where did the experience lose credibility?
  • Did the data make you feel like you were using a real product?

Key Takeaways

Validate Product Hypotheses via Risk-Based Prototyping

Product Managers must master the three ingredients of context injection: hypothesis, real data, and user voice, to build high-signal prototypes. This ensures the AI output reflects actual market conditions rather than a generic design.

PMs utilize the validation loop to move systematically through the stages of hypothesis, constraints, stress testing, and synthesis. This provides technical and usability evidence, compressing weeks of traditional development into a single session.

Product Managers should use the problem framework to articulate exactly what is being tested before wasting credits or prompting. Defining the what and why upfront prevents aimless building and ensures the resulting prototype serves as a precision instrument for validation.
