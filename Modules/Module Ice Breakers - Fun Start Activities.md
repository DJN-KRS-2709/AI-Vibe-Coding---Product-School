# Module Ice Breakers — Fun Start Activities

> **Purpose:** A 5–8 minute energizer at the start of each module. Sets the tone, primes the room for AI/vibe coding, and reminds everyone that this space moves fast and weird things happen weekly.
>
> **When to use:** Right after "Who Am I" (M1) or "Recap" (M2+) and before diving into core content. Keeps Block 1 punchy.

---

## How to Run It

| Module | Suggested Activity | Duration | Rationale |
|--------|--------------------|----------|-----------|
| **M1** | Who Said It? (short set) | 5 min | First impression — playful, sets "AI vs human" radar |
| **M2** | True or False: Did AI Really Do This? | 6 min | Bridges to "what are we actually testing?" |
| **M3** | Who Said It? (extended set) | 7 min | Deeper cuts before precision work |
| **M4** | Spot the AI Output | 6 min | Visual/execution lens — which build is AI? |
| **M5** | Who Said It? or Spot the AI Output | 5–7 min | Rotate based on cohort energy |
| **M6** | Quick lightning round (5 items) | 4 min | Keep it tight — ship day focus |

**Instructor tip:** Poll the room first. "Raise your hand if you think it's human." Then reveal. No need for slides — read aloud or share a simple slide deck.

## 3. Spot the AI Output — Which One Is AI?

*Show two options (code, copy, or design). Can students tell which is AI-generated?*

**When to use:** M4 or M5. Requires screens/slides. Good for cohorts that have built enough to recognize AI patterns.

### Option A: Code Snippets (2–3 pairs, ~6 min)

Show two code blocks side by side. Poll: Left or Right?

| # | Left Snippet (teaser) | Right Snippet (teaser) | Answer | Telltale Signs |
|---|----------------------|------------------------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | `// Comprehensive function to handle user validation...`<br>`function validateInput(userData) {` | `// checks the thing`<br>`function validate(u) {` | Left = AI | Left: "comprehensive," verbose, over-documented. Right: terse, human shortcuts. |
| 2 | `// Robust error handling with fallback logic`<br>`try { ... } catch (e) { console.log("Error:", e); }` | `// TODO: fix this properly someday`<br>`if (!x) return null;` | Left = AI | Left: "robust," generic. Right: TODO, pragmatic null check. |
| 3 | `# Elegant solution for data processing`<br>`def process_data(input_data):` | `# hacky but works`<br>`def proc(d):` | Left = AI | Left: "elegant," formal. Right: "hacky," abbreviated names. |

**Instructor script:**
> "Two code snippets. Which one do you think the AI wrote? [Poll.] [Reveal.] AI tends to be polite, verbose, and uses words like 'comprehensive' and 'robust.' Humans leave TODOs and abbreviate."

### Option B: Product Copy (2–3 pairs)

| # | Option A | Option B | Answer | Telltale Signs |
|---|----------|----------|--------|----------------|
| 1 | "A powerful platform designed to streamline your workflow and maximize productivity." | "The app that actually remembers where you left off." | A = AI | A: generic, "powerful," "streamline," "maximize." B: specific, concrete. |
| 2 | "Our solution leverages cutting-edge technology to deliver exceptional results." | "It just works. No setup. No hassle." | A = AI | A: buzzwords, passive. B: short, confident, human. |

**Instructor script:**
> "Two taglines. Which sounds like ChatGPT wrote it? [Poll.] AI loves 'leverage,' 'cutting-edge,' and 'exceptional.' Humans get to the point."

### Option C: Image Pairs (needs prep)

*Use Figma, DALL·E, or Midjourney outputs. Pair an AI-generated image with a human-made one (e.g., logo, UI mockup, illustration).*

| Pair Idea | Sources | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| Logo design | AI logo vs. designer logo | Look for: extra fingers, too-perfect symmetry, generic stock feel |
| UI mockup | v0/Lovable output vs. Figma from scratch | AI: perfectly aligned, sometimes generic components |
| Portrait/avatar | AI headshot vs. photo | AI: smooth skin, odd teeth, background artifacts |

**Prep:** Save 2–3 pairs before class. Add to slide deck. Reveal with "Left or Right?"

## 4. Living Doc — How to Keep This Fresh

### Recommended Sources to Monitor

| Source | What to Look For | Cadence |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| **r/ChatGPT, r/artificial** | Viral AI outputs, weird prompts, "AI said this" moments | Weekly |
| **X/Twitter** | AI researchers, product people, meme accounts (e.g. @emilymbender, @kaborobot) | Ongoing |
| **Tech news** | Wired, The Verge, Ars Technica — "AI did X" stories | Weekly |
| **Product School / your network** | Student submissions, "did you see when AI..." | After each cohort |
| **Hacker News** | "Show HN" AI tools, discussion threads | Weekly |

### What Makes a Good Addition

- **Who Said It?** — Quote is short, punchy, and genuinely ambiguous. The reveal should surprise.
- **True or False** — Statement sounds plausible either way. Source is real and verifiable.
- **Spot the AI Output** — Pair should be genuinely tricky. Telltale signs (verbose, generic, buzzwords) should be teachable.
- **Recency** — Prefer items from the last 6–12 months. Older classics (Turing, Musk 2014) stay; rotate the rest.

### Update Cadence

| Action | When |
|--------|------|
| Add 1–2 new Who Said It? quotes | Every 2–3 cohorts |
| Add 1–2 new True or False statements | Every 2–3 cohorts |
| Run Student-Submitted harvest (M6) | End of each cohort |
| Retire items that feel dated or overused | After 4–5 cohorts |
| Audit "Nano Banana" and similar claims | Before each run |

| Format | Description | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| **Headline or Hoax?** | Read a headline, true or fabricated? | Rotate with True/False |
| **One-Word Vibe Check** | "What word comes to mind when I say 'AI prototype'?" — quick round | M6, tight time |
| **Student-Submitted** | End of cohort: "What's the weirdest thing AI did for you this course?" → add to bank | After each cohort |

### Student-Submitted Process

1. **Last session (M6):** Ask: "What's the weirdest, funniest, or most surprising thing AI did for you in this course?"
2. **Collect:** Slack, Miro, or sticky notes. 1–2 sentences each.
3. **Curate:** Pick 2–3 that are generic enough (no internal product details) and funny/insightful.
4. **Add to bank:** Drop into Who Said It? or True/False. Tag: "From Cohort [X], [Date]."
5. **Next cohort:** Use them. Attribution optional: "A past student had AI do this..."
