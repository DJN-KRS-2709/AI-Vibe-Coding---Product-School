Three Main Ingredients

What Goes Into Context Injection

Design systems and components provide the shell. Context Injection provides the soul.
1 · Hypothesis
The strategic anchor
Defines the core strategic goal of your build in the opening prompt.

"I am testing whether…a guided walkthrough reduces user friction during initial onboarding."

2 · Real Data
Domain-specific evidence
Replaces generic placeholders with domain-specific information to test logic accuracy.

"Use this…CSV of 50 anonymized user logs to populate the retention risk dashboard."

3 · User Voice
Persona terminology
Injects specific tone and terminology so the prototype resonates with the target audience.

"Use the…direct, analytical tone of a Customer Success VP — focus on 'NRR' and 'Health Scores'."

02 — SECTION
The Prototype Validation Lens
Framework

The Validation Lens Loop

This cycle turns raw ideas into validated strategy. Each revolution sharpens your hypothesis and tightens constraints — moving your product from uncertain to evidence-backed.
01
Identify the Hypothesis
Name the test. If you can't complete "I'm testing whether [X]", you aren't ready to build.
The "why" that governs every prompt.
02
Define the Constraints
Set the guardrails — intent, logic, interaction patterns. Context injection of Data + User Voice.
Guardrails for the build.
03
Build and Stress-Test
Don't build to look pretty. Build to find where it breaks. Finding a break = highest signal.
Find logic and friction gaps now.
04
Synthesize and Pivot
Update your hypothesis with what you learned, then continue or pivot. Iterate strategy, not pixels.
Close the loop with learning.
What's Your Kill Switch?

Try to Kill Your Idea First

Ask: "Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing?" Once you have your answer — go build that first.
Risk TypeQuestionBuild This
Value"Will anyone actually want this?"Focus on user intent — build a landing page. Test whether the core premise solves a real problem or is just a novelty.
Usability"Will the experience make sense?"Focus on interaction — build a clickable mockup. Simplify complex steps and find where the flow breaks down.
Feasibility"Will the logic actually work?"Focus on constraints — build a functional app. Find edge cases where your logic or data might fail.
0 → 1 · New Products
Kill switch is usually desirability. Nobody wants it yet — prove they will. Test the problem before investing in build.
1 → N · Existing Products
Kill switch is adoption and flow. People have it; prove they'll use it. Screenshot what exists, build on top.

The Problem Framework

Six-Part Pre-Flight Checklist

Forces you to articulate what you're testing, not just what you're shipping. Fill this out before you open any tool.
01
Goal
What are we trying to achieve?
The outcome, not the output.
02
Problem
What's blocking us right now?
Name the obstacle precisely.
03
Context
Who, where, when?
The specifics that shape the solution space.
04
Constraints
What can't we change?
Technical limits, timelines, budgets.
05
Success Criteria
What does validation look like?
Define the threshold upfront.
06
Explore
What are we trying to learn?
This is the key. The learning objective.

Key Takeaways

Sharpen Intent with Strategic Prototypes

1
Master the three ingredients of context injection
Hypothesis, real data, user voice. Build high-signal prototypes that reflect actual market conditions, not generic design.
2
Use the validation loop to move systematically
Hypothesis → constraints → stress test → synthesis. Compress weeks of traditional development into a single session.

Use the problem framework before you prompt
Articulate exactly what's being tested. Defining the what and why upfront prevents aimless building.
