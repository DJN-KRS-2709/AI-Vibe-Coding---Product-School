# Instructor Demo Voiceover Guide

Pre-recorded screen captures for all instructor-led demos across Modules 1–6. The new instructor plays the video and narrates live using the voiceover script below. Pause the video at the marked points to add commentary or take questions.

**How to use this document:**
- Each demo has a **What the video shows** section describing the screen recording content
- The **Voiceover** sections are what the instructor says while the video plays
- **[PAUSE VIDEO]** markers indicate where to stop playback and talk to the room
- **[RESUME VIDEO]** markers indicate where to start playback again
- Timestamps are approximate — adjust to the actual recording

## Module 1, Demo 1: The Triple-Threat Build

"Here's the scenario. Enterprise customers are churning 30 days after onboarding. The VP of Customer Success thinks it's a UX issue. The Head of Product thinks you need better feature discovery. The CEO thinks we should add a self-serve analytics dashboard. Nobody has data. Everybody has opinions. Sound familiar? This is the reality of product work."

"I'm going to build three completely different prototypes from this one problem. Watch how fast this goes."

**Voiceover when done:** "Same problem. Completely different product. In four minutes, we've moved from a B2C user view to a B2B operations view. Both are valid. Neither is 'right' yet. But now we're comparing concrete options instead of arguing abstractions."

**Voiceover when done:** "Look at this. Account health scores, feature adoption by module, activity trends, at-risk accounts — all in a layout that looks like it belongs in a real product. And it took seven minutes."

## Module 1, Demo 2: Moving From Basic to Pro

**Slide:** 20 — Instructor-Led Demo: Moving From Basic to Pro
**Recording length:** ~15 minutes
**What the video shows:** Three upgrade passes on a prototype — design system matching with a Mobbin screenshot, adding interactivity (progress tracker, recommendation cards, loading skeleton), and surgical refinement of specific UI sections (header bar, help section).

### Setup — Before Pressing Play

"You have three working prototypes, but they all look like 'vibe' sketches. Your VP of Design won't look at them because they don't match the brand, and your Engineering Lead says they're too generic to be useful. Speed got you the idea. Now, strategic iteration must get you the buy-in."

"This is exactly what happened at Spotify when we prototyped new features — the first version always looks generic. What convinces stakeholders isn't more features, it's making it look and feel like it already belongs in the product. Let's take a look at how we can transform this."

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

### Step 1: Design System Matching (~5 min)

**What the video shows:** Uploading a Mobbin screenshot and pasting a detailed design transformation prompt — dark sidebar, modern cards, specific color palette, horizontal progress tracker, 2x2 action grid.

**Voiceover while generating:** "Watch the transformation. I'm not adding features. I'm applying a professional design system — dark sidebar, card layout, specific typography, consistent spacing. Same content, completely different credibility."

**Voiceover when done:** "Same dashboard. But now it looks like Linear or Notion. That visual upgrade is what gets you past the 'nice prototype' reaction and into 'when can we ship this?' territory."

### Step 2: Add Interactivity (~5 min)

**What the video shows:** Three rapid-fire prompts — interactive progress tracker with completed/current/locked states, actionable recommendation cards with hover and complete states, and a loading skeleton animation.

**Voiceover while generating:** "Now we're making it functional. The progress tracker has clickable states — completed, current, locked. The recommendation cards expand on click. And watch this — a loading skeleton. 1.5 seconds of perceived performance. Small detail, huge credibility boost."

**Voiceover when done:** "This isn't a screenshot anymore. It's an interactive experience. Filters work, cards expand, states change. That's the difference between 'looks like a product' and 'feels like a product.'"

### Step 3: Surgical Refinement (~5 min)

**What the video shows:** Two focused prompts — one fixing only the header bar (personalized greeting, notification bell, avatar), one fixing only the help section (gradient card, chat button, browse docs link). Nothing else changes.

**Voiceover while generating:** "This is the key skill — surgical steering. I'm fixing ONE section without rebuilding anything else. The header gets a personalized greeting and notification badge. The help section becomes an actual call-to-action. Two small prompts, massive polish."

**Voiceover when done:** "The key to pro-level vibe coding is compounding small improvements, not starting over every time the AI misses a detail."

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"We've just transformed a rough idea into a production-grade prototype through strategic iteration. While you're still building fast, you're doing so with direction and quality. In the next lab, you'll apply these same techniques to your own builds."

## Module 2, Demo: What You Can Prove with Context Injection

**Slide:** 6 — Instructor-Led Demo: What You Can Prove with Context Injection
**Recording length:** ~8 minutes
**What the video shows:** Side-by-side comparison of the M1 prototype (polished but hollow) versus a context-injected rebuild using real retention data, user quotes, and a named hypothesis. Then both versions shown in separate tabs.

### Setup — Before Pressing Play

"Last session was speed. You built fast and proved you can create functional prototypes. But today's question is different: are you building the right thing?"

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

### The Facade (~2 min)

**What the video shows:** Clicking through the M1 Retention Engine prototype — onboarding flow, dashboard, interactive components. All looking polished.

**Voiceover:** "This is the prototype from Module 1. Clean design, Mobbin-matched, interactive components — it looks like a real product. Pretty impressive for one session's work."

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"What assumption is this testing?" — Pause. Let the silence land. Wait 5-10 seconds.

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

**What the video shows:** Pointing to specific elements — hardcoded charts, placeholder copy, generic sample data.

**Voiceover:** "See these numbers? Where did they come from? Nowhere. They're hardcoded. These labels? Placeholder text. And these charts? Generic sample data that means nothing. If your VP asked 'what did you learn from this?' — what would you say? 'It looks nice'? That's not a product insight. That's interior design."

### Context Injection (~3 min)

**What the video shows:** Opening Lovable, pasting the mega-prompt with the B2B retention hypothesis, real data (30% churn, 68% vs 22% retention split, 12% Day-3 invite rate), user quotes, and constraints. Lovable generates a 3-screen onboarding flow plus experiment summary.

**Voiceover while generating:** "I'm rebuilding this with three ingredients: a hypothesis, real data, and real user voice. Watch what changes. Same tool. Same time. But this prompt has a hypothesis, real retention data, a named constraint, and actual user quotes baked in."

**Voiceover when done:** "Click through the three onboarding screens. See the retention data displayed on screen — 12% invite rate, 68% vs 22% retention. Real user quotes embedded in the UI. And an experiment plan with success criteria built right in."

### The Difference (~2 min)

**What the video shows:** Switching between both versions in separate browser tabs.

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"Point to the M1 version — placeholder data, hardcoded charts, generic copy. It looks like a product. But what question does it answer? None."

"Now point to the context-injected version — 12% Day-3 invite rate displayed on screen, real user quotes, a named hypothesis about surfacing invites during onboarding. It even has an experiment plan and success criteria. It's testing a specific assumption."

"Same tool. Same amount of time. The only difference is the inputs. Three ingredients: a hypothesis, real data, and user voice. The first one generates applause. The second one generates evidence. That's the shift we're making today."

## Module 3, Demo: The Precision Prompt Chain

**Slide:** 7 — Instructor-Led Demo: The Precision Prompt Chain
**Recording length:** ~6 minutes
**What the video shows:** Three sequential prompts applied to the M2 Retention Engine prototype — expanding from 3 screens to 5, adding loading/empty/error states, then surgically refining the PM Dashboard.

### Setup — Before Pressing Play

"This is our M2 build. Real data — 68% retain when they invite teammates, 22% when going solo. Real user quotes. A named hypothesis. Design-matched. Your VP looks at this and says 'Great. Where's the full flow? What happens when something goes wrong? Where does the PM track if this is working?' And you have nothing to show."

"We have three screens. No loading states. No error handling. No secondary views. And if I asked your teammate to reproduce this, they couldn't — because it was built with one big prompt. Three prompts. Watch what happens."

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

### Prompt 1 — Expand (~90 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting a chain-of-thought prompt that adds Screen 4 (Team Workspace — invited teammates, pending/accepted status, quick-start template) and Screen 5 (PM Dashboard — Day-3 invite rate, retention comparison, onboarding completion, users who haven't invited). Navigation logic wired between all screens.

**Voiceover while generating:** "Watch — I'm not rebuilding the 3 screens I already have. I'm expanding from them. The AI builds Screen 4 first, then Screen 5, in sequence. Each one depends on the last."

**Voiceover when done:** "3 screens became 5. Complete user journey — from onboarding through team setup to PM tracking. Navigation works. And I didn't touch the original 3 screens."

### Prompt 2 — Behavior (~90 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting a constraint grounding prompt that adds skeleton loading screens to Team Workspace and PM Dashboard, an empty state for no teammates, an error state for failed invites, and a connection-lost message for the dashboard.

**Voiceover while generating:** "This is the moment the prototype starts behaving like real software. Loading states, empty states, error handling — the unhappy paths that separate a mockup from a product."

**Voiceover when done:** "Same screens. But now they have loading skeletons, empty states, and error handling. Before this prompt, it was a mockup. Now it behaves like a shipped product. Your VP doesn't know the difference."

### Prompt 3 — Refine (~60 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting a reflection prompt targeting only the PM Dashboard — the AI lists 3 gaps, then adds a Nudge button next to non-inviters, makes the retention chart interactive with hover data, and adds a time-range toggle. No other screens change.

**Voiceover while generating:** "I'm refining ONE screen without rebuilding anything else. Surgical steering."

**Voiceover when done:** "I changed one screen. The other four are untouched. That's the shift. You're not starting over. You're directing — expand here, add states there, refine this. Like giving sprint tickets to an engineer."

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"3 prompts. 5 connected screens. Loading states, error handling, empty states. An interactive PM dashboard with a nudge button. And I didn't rebuild anything — I expanded what I had. You'll do this to your own build in the lab."

## Module 4, Demo: From Black Box to Production-Ready

**Slide:** 7 — Instructor-Led Demo: Comparing the Exterior With What's Under the Hood
**Recording length:** ~8 minutes
**What the video shows:** Starting with the messy code view of the M3 prototype, then running three prompts (refactor, extract Living PRD, generate engineering handoff), followed by connecting the backend via Lovable Cloud.

### Setup — Before Pressing Play

"Here's the problem we're solving. Your prototype looks like a finished product — high-fidelity visuals, solid hypothesis, interactive states. But if an engineer asked 'How does the data flow?' or 'Where is the logic for this screen?' — you'd be stuck digging through a messy file tree."

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

### Show the Black Box (~1 min)

**What the video shows:** The M3 Retention Engine in Lovable. Clicking through the polished screens, then switching to code view. The file tree shows generic names — `screen five`, `handleClick2`, everything in one file, no README.

**Voiceover:** "This is our M3 build. Five screens, design-system matched, interactive states, real user quotes. If your VP saw this, they'd think engineering built it."

"Now look at the code. `screen five`. `handleClick2`. All logic in one file. No README. No documentation. This is what the engineer who inherits your prototype actually sees. This is the black box."

### Prompt 1 — Refactor the Code (~60 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the refactor prompt. The file tree transforms — component names change, a `/services` folder appears, a README generates.

**Voiceover while generating:** "Watch the file tree on the left."

**Voiceover when done:** "`Component1` is now `TeamValueScreen`. `Component3` is now `InviteFlow`. There's a `/services` folder for data logic. There's a README. An engineer can read this on day one. Same product — readable code."

### Prompt 2 — Extract the Living PRD (~60 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the Living PRD extraction prompt. A `livingprd.md` file appears with Product Overview, Problem & Hypothesis, User Flow, Technical Reality, and more.

**Voiceover:** Let the class watch it generate in silence.

**Voiceover when done:** "I didn't write this. The prototype wrote its own spec. The hypothesis — 'surfacing team invites during first-run onboarding increases Day-3 invite rate from 12% to 25%' — extracted from what we built, not imagined before building."

### Prompt 3 — Engineering Handoff (~40 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the handoff prompt. An `engineeringhandoff.md` file generates with component list, data model, top 3 technical decisions, and a 'start here' guide.

**Voiceover when done:** "A new engineer joins the team Monday. They open this document. In 5 minutes, they know what the onboarding flow does, what's real, what's mocked, and where to start. That's the handoff."

### Connect the Backend (~30 sec)

**What the video shows:** Prompting Lovable to add a database feature. Lovable Cloud prompts to enable. Click Allow. Database, auth, storage spin up. The Lovable Cloud dashboard shows real tables.

**Voiceover:** "Your prototype has a real database, real auth, and real storage. This isn't a toy anymore. This is real infrastructure."

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"Same prototype. Same onboarding flow. Same hypothesis. But now it has clean code, a Living PRD, an engineering handoff, GitHub from Module 2 with refactored code, and a real backend. You'll do all of this to your own build in the labs."

## Module 5, Demo: From Connected Infrastructure to Secured Product

**Slide:** 7 — Instructor-Led Demo: From Connected Infrastructure to Secured Product
**Recording length:** ~8 minutes
**What the video shows:** Starting with the M4 build showing hardcoded dashboard data, then running three prompts — wiring all data to the database, adding auth with Row-Level Security, and handling edge cases. Then stress-testing with DevTools offline mode and deploying to a live URL.

### Setup — Before Pressing Play

"Let's take a look at our M4 build. Clean code, Living PRD, GitHub repo since Module 2, Lovable Cloud connected. The infrastructure is real. But look at the product sitting on top of it. The Day-3 invite rate? Hardcoded at 38%. No matter what happens. Login users act the same. We see them in the backend stored. They all have the same experience."

**[RESUME VIDEO]**

### Prompt 1 — Wire All Data to the Database (~90 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the schema expansion prompt — creates an invites table, replaces hardcoded metrics with real queries. Going through the onboarding flow, inviting a teammate, then checking the dashboard — the rate updates from real data.

**Voiceover while generating:** "I'm not changing the UI. I'm changing where the data comes from. Every metric on that dashboard will be a real calculation from the database, not a hardcoded number."

**Voiceover when done:** "The dashboard is now calculating real numbers from real data — not hardcoded values. That percentage isn't a placeholder anymore. It's a live calculation from the invites table."

### Prompt 2 — Auth + Row-Level Security (~90 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the auth prompt. Login/signup screen appears. Signing up as sarah@acme.com, completing onboarding, inviting a teammate. Logging out. Signing up as mike@acme.com — fresh onboarding flow, no Sarah data visible. Dashboard shows aggregate data from both users.

**Voiceover while generating:** "Auth is enabled from Module 4. But there's no login screen and no data isolation. Watch — this is where users become real."

**Voiceover when done:** "Different user, different experience. Sarah completed onboarding and invited a teammate. Mike just signed up — he sees the onboarding flow fresh. Sarah can't see Mike's data. Mike can't see Sarah's. That's Row-Level Security — the database enforces who sees what. This isn't a prototype anymore — it's a multi-user product."

### Prompt 3 — Edge Cases (~60 sec)

**What the video shows:** Pasting the edge case prompt. Then opening DevTools, going to Network, setting Offline — the app shows an error message with a retry option instead of freezing. Unchecking Offline — it recovers. Trying to invite the same email twice — duplicate message appears.

**Voiceover:** "Now watch what happens when I kill the connection."

**Voiceover when done:** "Instead of silence, the user sees an error message with a retry option. I tried inviting the same person twice — it caught the duplicate. That's the difference between connected infrastructure and a resilient product. Real products break — yours should break gracefully."

### Deploy (~30 sec)

**What the video shows:** Clicking Share → Publish in Lovable. The URL appears. Opening it in a browser — the login screen loads. Opening the URL on a phone. Signing up as a new user on the phone. Going back to the laptop — the phone user's data appears in the dashboard.

**Voiceover:** "One button. That's it. No CI/CD pipeline, no server configuration, no DNS records."

**Voiceover when done:** "That URL is live. Right now. On the internet. I just signed up on my phone. Went through onboarding. And here it is — on a different device, in the retention dashboard. Two real users, one shared database, one live URL. That's not a prototype. That's a product."

**[PAUSE VIDEO]**

"Same prototype. Same hypothesis. Three prompts turned connected infrastructure into a secured, resilient product. All data is real, users are isolated, failures are handled. And it's live on the internet. You'll do this to your own build in the labs."

## Module 6, Demo: From User Clicks to Actionable Insights

**Note:** Module 6 does not have a formal pre-recorded demo slide in the PPTX. The instructor narrates the Lovable Insights panel live on their own deployed project. This section provides the talk track for walking through that data.

**Slide:** Following Slide 5 (Shift From Shipping to Proving With Data), before the first lab
**Timing:** ~3 minutes, delivered live (no recording needed)

### Talk Track

"Your product has been live for a few days. Real users have been clicking around. But how do you actually know if it's solving the problem you identified in Module 2? You have a hunch. Maybe you tested it yourself. But that's not data — that's confirmation bias. You need the hard facts."

"Let me show you Lovable Insights. You get this for free in Lovable. It's been collecting data since the moment we deployed in Module 5. No setup. No extra tools. It's just there."

Open the Insights panel on the deployed Retention Engine project.

"Look at this — these are real sessions from real users. I can see which screens they visited, which features they clicked, and how many came back for a second session."

"My hypothesis in Module 2 was that surfacing team invites during first-run onboarding would increase the Day-3 invite rate. But if the data shows users aren't getting to the invite step, that hypothesis has a problem. I might need a V2 — or a pivot."

"In a few minutes, you're going to open your own Insights and we'll figure out what your data is actually telling you."
