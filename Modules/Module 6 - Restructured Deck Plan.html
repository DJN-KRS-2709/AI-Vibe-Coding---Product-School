INSTRUCTOR DEMO

Two ways to see what's happening on your deployed product:

1. Lovable Insights — Built-in analytics. Page views, sessions, feature usage.
2. Google Analytics (GA4) — Industry-standard. Traffic sources, user flows, bounce rates, funnels.

Watch: I open my deployed Retention Engine. I pull up the insights panel. Then I show what GA4 adds on top.

The data is already being collected. You just need to look at it.
5 min
Speaker Notes

Pull up the deployed Retention Engine. Open Lovable insights — show metrics. Then show GA4 dashboard: real-time view, user flow, engagement. "Your product has been live since M5. People have been using it. Did you know this data existed?" Don't go deep on GA4 setup — the lab covers that.

4
Teaching
What to Measure
Focus on four categories:

Engagement: How many signed up? Completed the core flow? Which features used most?
Retention: Do users come back? Where do they drop off?
Quality: Page load time, error rates, mobile vs desktop
Impact: Does user behavior match your M2 hypothesis?

The metric that matters most: does user behavior confirm or contradict your original problem statement?
3 min
Speaker Notes

"You don't need 50 metrics. You need 3–5 that map to your problem statement. If your M2 hypothesis was 'PMs will track invite status weekly' — your key metric is: do they? The engagement numbers tell you if people use it. The impact question tells you if it's solving the problem." Framework, not lecture. 3 minutes.

Lab 1 — Connect Analytics

15 min
5
Lab Part 1
Set Up Tracking on Your Product
Step 1 (5 min): Open your deployed product's Lovable insights panel. Review what's tracked. Screenshot your baseline.

Step 2 (5 min): Set up Google Analytics. Create a GA4 property. Get the Measurement ID. Prompt Lovable:
"Add Google Analytics tracking to this project. Use Measurement ID: G-XXXXXXXXXX. Add the gtag script to the head of the main HTML file. Track page views automatically."
Redeploy after adding the tag.

Step 3 (5 min): Verify — open your product, check GA4 real-time view. You should see yourself as a live user.

If GA4 takes too long, Lovable insights alone is enough for today.

If stuck on GA4, Lovable insights alone is enough. After 15 min: "Who can see themselves in the real-time view? That's your product, being measured, right now."

AI-Powered Product Analysis

10 min
6
Teaching
The PM's New Superpower: AI + Data + Problem Statement
Traditional: export → spreadsheet → charts → doc → days.
AI-powered: metrics + problem statement → AI → evaluation in minutes.

The formula:
1. Pull your product metrics
2. Pull your problem statement from your Living PRD
3. Pull your M2 hypothesis
4. Feed all three to ChatGPT or Gemini

The AI doesn't replace your judgment. It accelerates your analysis.
5 min
Speaker Notes

"This is the most advanced PM skill in the course. You're not asking AI to build something — you're asking it to think with you. Feed it three things: problem statement, hypothesis, actual data. The AI synthesizes what would take you hours. It flags patterns you might miss. But YOU make the decision. AI accelerates analysis. You own the judgment."

Lab 2 — AI Analysis Sprint

15 min
8
Lab Part 2
Analyze Your Product with AI
Step 1 (3 min): Open insights or GA4. Screenshot/copy your key metrics.

Step 2 (2 min): Open your Living PRD (from M4). Copy problem statement + M2 hypothesis.

Step 3 (5 min): Open ChatGPT or Gemini. Use this prompt:
"I built a product to solve this problem: [problem statement]. My hypothesis was: [hypothesis]. Here are the metrics from my deployed product: [paste metrics]. Evaluate: is this product solving the problem? What does the data suggest? What are the top 3 things I should improve, ranked by likely impact?"
Step 4 (5 min): Read the AI analysis. Highlight the single highest-impact recommendation.
15 min
Speaker Notes

Hardest part: finding the problem statement. If they can't find their Living PRD, a one-sentence problem statement from memory is fine. After 15 min: "What did AI tell you? Anyone surprised?" Get 2–3 students to share. The learning moment: data told them something they didn't expect about their own product.

Iteration — Evidence Over Guesses

15 min
9
Teaching
The Iteration Prompt: Evidence-Based Improvement
Most PMs iterate by instinct: "I think the onboarding is confusing."
Evidence-based: "Users drop off at step 3. The CTA is unclear. Rewrite step 3."

The Iteration Prompt Pattern:
"Based on this analysis: [paste AI findings]. The highest-impact improvement is: [specific finding]. Update the product to address this: [describe the change]. Keep everything else working."
This is different from M1–M5 prompts. Not building new features — improving existing ones based on evidence.

Spotify calls this "tweak it" — the fifth stage: understand it, think it, build it, ship it, tweak it.
5 min
Speaker Notes

"This is the mindset shift. In M1–M5 you were building — adding features. Now you're improving based on evidence. The iteration prompt is different: not 'add X' but 'the data shows Y is broken, fix it like this.' Spotify calls it 'tweak it.' The best PMs spend more time here than in any other stage."

10
Lab Part 3
Build One Data-Driven Improvement
Pick the single highest-impact finding from your AI analysis. Build it.

Step 1 (2 min): Write your iteration prompt using the pattern above.
Step 2 (5 min): Paste into Lovable. Review the changes. Test.
Step 3 (3 min): Redeploy. Your live URL now reflects a data-driven improvement.

You just completed the full product lifecycle: understand → hypothesize → build → ship → measure → learn → improve.
10 min
Speaker Notes

ONE change. The temptation is to fix everything — resist it. The discipline of prioritization is the skill. After 10 min: "Who redeployed? What did you change and why?" Every answer should reference the data that drove the decision.
