Visual Reference Guide

Your screenshot is the single highest-ROI input for your prototype. Show the AI what you want — don't describe it. This guide shows you where to find one and what makes a good reference.

The difference between a generic AI demo and a VP-ready prototype is visual context.
A single screenshot replaces 20 lines of prompting.

🔍
Mobbin
Free. Best source for most students.
A curated library of real product screens — onboarding flows, dashboards, settings pages, marketplaces. Search by pattern (e.g., "onboarding invite"), not by product name. You'll find screens from products that solved the same UX problem you're working on.
How to use it
  1. Go to mobbin.com (free account)
  2. Search for the pattern, not the product: "team invite flow", "analytics dashboard", "provider profile", "CRM call logging"
  3. Browse results. Find a screen that's close to what you want — layout, information density, visual style
  4. Screenshot it (Cmd+Shift+4 on Mac, Win+Shift+S on Windows)
  5. Drop the screenshot into your Lovable prompt with: "Match this look and feel"
SUGGESTED SEARCHES BY SCENARIO
Retention: "team invite onboarding" Internal Tool: "CRM call log mobile" Marketplace: "provider profile reviews" Dashboard: "analytics dashboard simple"
📱
Your Existing Product
Highest fidelity. Best for 1-to-N scenarios.
If you're working on an existing product (and most of you are), screenshot what you already have. Your prototype should look like it belongs in your actual product — not like a standalone demo. The AI matches the layout, typography, spacing, and color language automatically.
How to use it
  1. Open the page/screen in your product that's closest to what you're building
  2. Screenshot the full page (not just a section)
  3. In your prompt: "This is our current [page name]. Build [the new feature] that fits naturally into this existing design"
  4. The AI will match your product's visual language — colors, spacing, component style
Best for
  • Adding a feature to an existing product (invite flow, new dashboard view, profile redesign)
  • "Bring Your Own" scenario students — this is your primary source
  • Scenarios 1, 2, 4 — these are all existing products with adoption problems
⚡
Competitor or Inspiration
Good when you know the benchmark.
If you know a product that nailed the pattern you're exploring, screenshot their implementation. This isn't copying — it's giving the AI a design vocabulary to work from. You'll customize with your own data and hypothesis.
Examples by scenario
  1. Retention Engine: Screenshot Slack's first-run invite flow, Linear's onboarding
  2. Internal Tool: Screenshot Gong's call summary, Notion's quick capture
  3. Marketplace: Screenshot Airbnb's provider profile, Thumbtack's trust badges
  4. Dashboard: Screenshot Mixpanel's guided view, Amplitude's custom dashboards
✏️
Whiteboard or Sketch
Surprisingly effective for layout intent.
A photo of a whiteboard sketch or a quick hand-drawn wireframe tells the AI about your intended layout and hierarchy — what goes where, what's prominent, what's secondary. The output won't look hand-drawn — the AI translates your intent into clean UI.
What Makes a Good Reference
Check these before dropping your screenshot into the prompt.
  • ✓ Full page, not a cropped section. The AI needs the full layout context — header, nav, content area, spacing.
  • ✓ Shows real content, not empty states. A screenshot with data in it produces better results than a blank template.
  • ✓ Matches the pattern you're testing. Building an invite flow? Use a screenshot of an invite flow — not a generic dashboard.
  • ✓ Clean capture. No browser chrome, no notification popups, no cursor visible. Clean screenshots produce clean output.
  • ✓ One screenshot per prompt. Don't overload with 5 references. One clear reference beats three ambiguous ones.
Don't do this
  • Screenshot a marketing homepage and expect a product interface
  • Use a mobile screenshot when you're building a desktop app
  • Describe the screenshot in words instead of attaching it — show, don't tell
  • Skip the visual reference entirely — this is the biggest missed opportunity
Do this instead
  • Use a product screen from the same domain as your scenario
  • Match the device type (desktop screenshot for desktop prototype)
  • Attach the image and say "Match this look and feel, then add [your feature]"
  • Combine: screenshot for layout + your data pack for content
3m
You have 3 minutes. Open Mobbin or screenshot your product. Find one reference that matches the pattern you're testing. Drop it in Slack with your Validation Brief. Then you're ready to build.
