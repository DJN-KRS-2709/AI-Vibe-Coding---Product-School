Module 1 — Pre-Read

Read this before your Module 1 session (live or async). Five minutes. It removes the setup friction so you can start building in the first ten minutes of class instead of the last ten.

What Module 1 is — and is not

Module 1 is the first time most PMs walk out of a session holding a live, clickable, shareable URL they made themselves. Not a Figma mockup. Not a slide. A working prototype. That's the experience.

It is not a Lovable tutorial. The tool changes; the methodology doesn't. By the end of Module 1 you'll have named the durable framework — the Confidence Line — and you'll know exactly where your build lives on it.

You will:

  • Build two prototypes from scratch.
  • Iterate one of them from vibe sketch to credible tool.
  • Show and swap with a peer after Lab 1 (clarity) · Swap and Review with a NEW peer after Lab 2 (credibility).
  • Commit your artifacts to 01-velocity/.
  • Name the Confidence Line — the framework that runs through every remaining module.

You will not:

  • Watch slides for an hour before opening the tool.
  • Wait for engineering to validate your idea.
  • Need to know any code.

What to set up before class (10 minutes total)

1. Lovable Pro access (5 min)

Your Product School partnership includes 3 months of Lovable Pro, free.

  • Go to lovable.dev and sign up if you haven't already.
  • Apply your Product School Pro code (sent in your cohort welcome email).
  • Confirm you have ~100 credits available in your dashboard.

If you can't find your code, ping students@productschool.com directly — not during the session, because we will not pause class for setup.

2. A free LLM for prompt drafting (3 min)

You'll plan your prompts outside Lovable to save credits. Have one of these open in a tab:

  • ChatGPT (free tier is fine)
  • Claude.ai (free tier is fine)
  • Gemini (free)

Any of them works. Pick whichever you're already comfortable with.

3. A Mobbin account (2 min, optional but recommended)

For Lab 2's design-match path. Free signup at mobbin.com. You'll grab a screenshot of a real product (e.g., Linear, Notion, Stripe) and feed it to Lovable as a design reference. If you skip this, you can still use any other screenshot — your own product, a competitor's, anything.

4. The lab guide (1 min)

Open Modules/M1 - Lab 1 Guide.html once before class so you've seen the four scenarios. You don't need to pick one yet. (Lab 2 guide opens after the break.)

The Confidence Line in 60 seconds

This is the framework you'll spend the rest of the certification on. Read it now so it lands instantly when it shows up at the end of Module 1.

M1–M2: High Ambiguity   →   M3–M4: Gaining Clarity   →   M5–M6: Production Confidence
   Build to think              From vibe to structure          Live URL + measured iteration
  • High Ambiguity (M1–M2). You don't know what to build. So you build fast, on instinct, to find out. Three rough prototypes beat one polished one.
  • Gaining Clarity (M3–M4). Direction is clearer. You shift from exploring to refining — precision prompting, living specs.
  • Production Confidence (M5–M6). You harden the prototype. Real APIs, real auth, real deployment. You ship.

Module 1 lives entirely in Phase 1. The reason building feels fast (and a bit messy) is because that's the point. Don't try to be in Phase 2 yet.

The scenario you'll be solving (preview)

In the live session you'll see this on slide 4:

Enterprise customers are churning 30 days after onboarding. The VP of Customer Success thinks it's a UX problem. The Head of Product thinks it's feature-discovery. The CEO wants a self-serve analytics dashboard. Nobody has data. Everybody has opinions.

You'll watch a demo where the instructor builds three competing prototypes from this one problem. Then you'll do the same — but with a scenario you pick from the lab guide.

Pick a scenario you'd want to keep building on. You'll carry this scenario through the entire rest of the course as the basis for your final project. You're not locked in yet (M2 is where you commit), but starting close to what you actually care about saves you a pivot later.

The four options:

  • Retention Engine — B2B SaaS, 30% churn at 90 days. (Best default if you can't decide.)
  • Internal Tool Nobody Uses — CRM with 18% adoption.
  • Marketplace Trust — Bookings flat, zero-review providers stuck.
  • Dashboard Nobody Reads — Analytics, 60% bounce rate.

If you have a real problem from your day job that fits one of these shapes, swap it in. The methodology is the same.

Voice + format reminders

  • Builds are individual, reviews are paired. The builds themselves are solo — no group dependencies on the artifact, no team coordination. But every lab is followed by a 10-minute paired Show and Swap (silent walk on a partner's prototype, then discuss three questions). Round 2 pairs you with a different partner from Round 1.
  • Async-friendly. If you're joining a live cohort, cameras on (it doubles your engagement). If you're going async, everything ships the same way — commit artifacts to your repo, post one-line learnings to #cohort-channel.
  • Direct, not cheerful. Real numbers, real examples, real companies. We'll talk about Slack pivoting from gaming to chat, Dropbox launching with a screencast, Airbnb's three air mattresses. No "imagine an app like…" hypotheticals.

What to bring to the session

  • Lovable Pro access confirmed.
  • One LLM (ChatGPT / Claude / Gemini) open in a tab.
  • The lab guide open in a second tab.
  • A screenshot of any real product you want to design-match in Lab 2 (Mobbin is the easy default).
  • A scenario from the four above (or your own version of one).

That's it. See you at the start.

After Module 1

You'll leave with three artifacts in 01-velocity/:

01-velocity/
├── prototype-v1.md            # Your Lab 1 build (URL + screenshot + assumption tested)
├── prototype-v2.md            # Your Lab 2 refinement (URL + before/after + 1–5 score)
└── confidence-line-reflection.md   # One-paragraph reflection on Phase 1

This is the first folder of the project repo you'll carry across all six modules. By Module 6, this folder structure is the certification — six folders, a live URL, a Validation Evidence Brief, a Living PRD, a Prompt Library, and an Engineering Handoff.

You're not building a course project. You're building a portfolio piece. Treat it that way.
