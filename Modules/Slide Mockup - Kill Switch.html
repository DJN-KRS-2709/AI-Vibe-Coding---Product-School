What's Your Kill Switch?
Three risk categories determine what you build. Your risk type isn't a philosophy — it's a build decision. The kill-switch question forces clarity: Which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing? Build to test that one first.
Risk Type The Question Build This
Value Will anyone want this? Landing page
Usability Will the experience make sense? Clickable mockup
Feasibility Will the logic actually work? Functional app
0-to-1 Products
Kill switch: desirability
Nobody wants it yet — prove they will. You're testing whether the problem is real before you invest in building.
1-to-N Products
Kill switch: adoption
People have it — prove they'll use it. You already have users, data, and a product. You never start from scratch. Screenshot what exists, feed it in, build on top.

Speaker Notes

On the table: "This is your decision tree. If your biggest risk is 'does anyone want this' — don't build an app. Build a landing page and see if people click. If the risk is 'will it make sense' — build a clickable mockup and watch someone try it. If it's 'will the logic work' — build a functional prototype. Match fidelity to risk."

On 0-to-1 vs 1-to-N: "Here's the shift most people miss. If you're building something brand new, the kill switch is almost always desirability — nobody wants it yet, prove they will. But most of you work on existing products. You're in 1-to-N territory. Your kill switch is adoption — people already have it, but they're not using the new thing you're adding."

On never starting from scratch (1-to-N): "And here's the kicker for 1-to-N — you never have to start from a blank page. You have an existing product. Screenshot it. Feed it into Lovable. Build on top of what already exists. That's the Screenshot-to-App entry point from slide 3. Your prototype should look like it belongs in your actual product, not like a standalone demo. That's what makes it testable."

Connect to scenarios: "Look at your scenario. The Retention Engine — that's 1-to-N, the product exists. The Internal Tool — also 1-to-N, CRM exists, adoption is the problem. If you picked one of these, your first move should be screenshotting what you already have."
