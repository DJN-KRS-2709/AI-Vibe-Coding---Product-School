Choose Your Scenario

Before the lab begins · 2 min

Pick the scenario you'll carry through Modules 2–6. No switching after today. This is your final project starting point.

Scenario 1
The Retention Engine
B2B SaaS, 30% churn in 90 days. Team invites matter. What's the real blocker?
Scenario 2
The Internal Tool Nobody Uses
CRM, 18% adoption. Reps hate it. CEO wants Gong. Fix adoption without replacing the tool.
Scenario 3
The Marketplace Trust Problem
Bookings flat. Zero-review providers. Solve cold-start trust.
Scenario 4
The Dashboard Nobody Reads
Analytics, 60% bounce. Power users love it, new users flee. Serve both.
💡
Option 5 — Bring Your Own: A real problem from your company. Needs a clear user, a measurable outcome, and enough ambiguity to test. If you can't decide between scenarios, pick 1 or 4 — they work for almost any PM role.

1

Fill Out Your Validation Brief

Lab Part 1, Step 1 · 5 minutes
⛔
Do NOT open Lovable yet. You're preparing the inputs that will make your build dramatically better than M1. The discipline of framing before building is the M2 skill.

Open the Prototype Builder tool — Step 1 walks you through your Validation Brief:

🔧 Open Prototype Builder — Step 1: Frame
Scenario selected — which of the 4 scenarios (or Bring Your Own)?
Hypothesis written — "If we [intervention], then [outcome], because [evidence]."
Risk type identified — Value (will anyone want this?), Usability (will it make sense?), or Feasibility (will the logic work?)
Kill switch named — which assumption, if wrong, kills the whole thing?
Success criteria defined — what does "validated" vs. "invalidated" look like?
Fidelity level chosen — landing page, clickable flow, or functional app?
💡
Stuck on the hypothesis? Ask: "What would kill this idea if we learned it was wrong?" That's your kill switch — and your hypothesis is the opposite of it.
2

Gather Your Evidence

Lab Part 1, Step 2 · 4 minutes

Click "Next: Gather Evidence" in the Prototype Builder. Your scenario data is loaded automatically. You're getting three types of real data:

📦 Open Prototype Builder — Step 2: Evidence
A
Read the user & stakeholder quotes
8–12 real-sounding quotes from interviews, surveys, and support tickets. Highlight 2–3 quotes that directly relate to your hypothesis. These go into your prompt.
B
Scan the metrics
15–20 rows of realistic quantitative data: retention curves, adoption rates, conversion funnels. Circle 3–5 data points you want your prototype to display. Real numbers, not placeholder text.
C
Review the scenario brief
The full problem context with competing stakeholder hypotheses. This is what gives your prototype grounding in reality.
⚠️
Don't include everything. Pick the 3–5 data points that matter most for your specific hypothesis. More data ≠ better prototype. Focused data = better prototype.

Build With Context

Lab Part 2 · 25 minutes

Click "Next: Build Your Prompt" in the Prototype Builder. Your hypothesis, evidence, and constraints are already assembled. Review, add your visual reference, then copy into Lovable.

🔧 Open Prototype Builder — Step 3: Build
1
Context — Paste your scenario brief
In the Prompt Builder
Paste the scenario brief from your Data Pack. Include 2–3 user quotes: "Here's what users are saying: [quote 1], [quote 2]." This grounds the AI in your problem domain.
2
Reference — Describe your visual anchor
In the Prompt Builder
Reference your screenshot: "Match this look and feel." You'll attach the actual image when you paste into Lovable.
3
Constraint — Add specific metrics
In the Prompt Builder
"Display these specific metrics: [metric 1], [metric 2], [metric 3]. Use these actual numbers, not placeholder data." Add guardrails: max screens, what to prioritize, what to avoid.
4
Output — Define what the tool should produce
In the Prompt Builder
"Build a [2–3 screen] flow that tests whether [your hypothesis]. Include: [screen 1], [screen 2], [screen 3]."
5
Copy → Paste into Lovable → Build
Remaining time
Copy the assembled prompt from the Builder. Open Lovable. Attach your screenshot. Paste the prompt. Build. Iterate if needed — but focus on testing the hypothesis, not making it pretty.

Your prompt should look like this:

Context
Build a [product type] for [audience]. [1–2 sentence problem statement]. Here's what users are saying: "[quote 1]" "[quote 2]" "[quote 3]"
Reference
Match this look and feel. [screenshot attached]
Constraint
Display these specific metrics: [metric 1: value], [metric 2: value], [metric 3: value]. Use these actual numbers. Prioritize [key flow]. Avoid generic patterns. Max [N] screens.
Output
Build a [N]-screen flow that tests whether [hypothesis]. Screen 1: [what it shows]. Screen 2: [what it shows]. Screen 3: [what it shows].
💰
Credits reminder: Plan your prompt in the Builder first — don't iterate inside Lovable. If a prompt fails twice, eject to ChatGPT to debug — don't burn credits.

Show and Swap Your Prototype

Breakout · 10 minutes · NEW partner

Pair up with a peer and experience their build as a real user. Test whether your strategic intent and kill switch actually translated into the build.
You will be assigned a new partner the moment the lab timer hits zero — that's why your shareable link has to be ready.

1
Swap prototype links in the chat
30 seconds
Drop your Lovable share link. Grab your partner's. No verbal context given or received — let the build speak.
2
Explore silently for 3 minutes
3 minutes
Click as if you've never seen it. If the user gets lost — let them stay lost. That's your data. Take notes against the three questions below.
3
Discuss — then swap roles and repeat
6 minutes (3 + 3)
Walk the three credibility questions with your partner. Be specific — vague feedback doesn't move the build. Then swap and run the loop on yours.

The three credibility questions (verbatim from the legacy walkthrough)

1
What do you think the core hypothesis or kill switch was?
2
Where did the experience lose credibility?
3
Did the data make you feel like you were using a real product?
🥶
Let them stay stuck. If your partner can't find a feature or doesn't understand a flow, that is the data. Don't rescue them with explanations — note where they got stuck and discuss it after the silent walk.

The question isn't "is this cool?"
It's "does this test what you said you were testing?"
